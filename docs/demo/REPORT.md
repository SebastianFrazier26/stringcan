# stringcan demo report

Generated by `cargo run -p stringcan-sim --bin report`. Every number below comes from the real engine (not a mock), audited for money conservation after each settlement. Processor fees are set to zero here so the arithmetic is easy to follow; the fee section at the end shows the gross-up.

Period 1 is always a bootstrap: whoever subscribes first is the only committed person and funds it alone (design doc, section 5). The interesting rows start at period 2, and the large credit in period 2 is the bootstrap prepayers getting back what they paid before the crowd had arrived.

## 4. A joiner before vs after the period is covered

The example from the product idea: 10 subscribers, 10.00 upkeep, so 1.00 each.

|---|---:|---:|
| renewer 1 | 3 | $1.00 |
| renewer 2 | 3 | $1.00 |
| renewer 3 | 3 | $1.00 |
| renewer 4 | 3 | $1.00 |
| NEW JOINER | 3 | $0.91 |
| renewer 5 | 3 | $0.91 |

| renewer 7 | 3 | $0.91 |
| renewer 8 | 3 | $0.91 |
| renewer 9 | 3 | $0.91 |

The joiner pays the lower price (they count themselves) and the price drops for the six renewers still to pay. When the period settles every one of the 11 participants has a net share of $0.90 to $0.91 (the 10.00 splits 11 ways with a remainder), and the early payers get $0.37 back in total as credit:

- renewer 1: $0.09 credit
- renewer 2: $0.09 credit
- renewer 3: $0.09 credit
- renewer 4: $0.09 credit
- renewer 10: $0.01 credit

## 5. Processor fees are part of the cost

The charge is grossed up (Stripe-like 2.9% + 0.30) so the pool receives the share exactly; the owner never absorbs fees and stringcan never keeps the difference.
